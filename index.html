8 IDIOMS
01

get the wrong end of the stick

UKINFORMAL

误解了事情；会错意

  1. I think you got the wrong end of the stick; we are postponing the launch, not cancelling it.

    我想你误会了，我们是推迟上线，不是取消它。

  2. He got the wrong end of the stick from one email and thought every team had to attend.

    他从一封邮件里会错了意，以为所有团队都必须参加。

02

go the whole nine yards

USINFORMAL

全力以赴；把所有该做的都做完

  1. If we rebrand, we should go the whole nine yards and update the onboarding too.

    如果要重新品牌化，我们就该彻底做到底，连入职流程也一起更新。

  2. The team went the whole nine yards for the conference, from the demo to live support.

    团队为这场会议做足了全套准备，从演示到现场支持都有安排。

03

not be out of the woods

USINFORMAL

尚未脱离困境

  1. The server is back online, but we are not out of the woods yet.

    服务器恢复在线了，但我们还没有完全脱离困境。

  2. Sales have improved, but the business is not out of the woods until cash flow stabilises.

    销售有所改善，但在现金流稳定前，公司还没真正脱险。

04

have a bee in your bonnet

UKINFORMAL

对某件事过度执着；一直念念不忘

  1. She has a bee in her bonnet about simplifying the approval process.

    她一直执着于简化审批流程这件事。

  2. He has had a bee in his bonnet about remote work all week.

    他这周一直对远程办公这件事念念不忘。

05

a bone of contention

UK / USFORMAL / NEUTRAL

引发争议的症结

  1. Salary remains the main bone of contention in the contract talks.

    薪资仍然是合同谈判中最主要的争议点。

  2. Data ownership became a bone of contention between the two companies.

    数据所有权成为两家公司之间的争议焦点。

06

bear fruit

UK / USFORMAL

产生成果；开始见效

  1. The mentoring programme is finally starting to bear fruit.

    导师计划终于开始见效了。

  2. Years of investment in automation are now bearing fruit.

    多年来对自动化的投入现在正在产生成果。

07

strike while the iron is hot

UK / USINFORMAL

趁热打铁；抓住时机

  1. Interest is high after the demo, so let’s strike while the iron is hot.

    演示后大家兴趣很高，我们要趁热打铁。

  2. They struck while the iron was hot and expanded into an adjacent market.

    他们抓住时机，迅速扩展到了相邻市场。

08

take the bull by the horns

UK / USINFORMAL

直面困难；果断处理棘手问题

  1. Rather than delay again, we need to take the bull by the horns and fix the security flaw.

    与其再拖延，我们得直面问题，修复这个安全漏洞。

  2. She took the bull by the horns and called the client herself.

    她果断接手，亲自给客户打了电话。

8 IDIOMS
01

get down to brass tacks

USINFORMAL

直击关键问题；开始认真处理实质内容

  1. We have discussed the background for long enough; let’s get down to brass tacks.

    背景已经讨论得够久了，我们开始谈实质问题吧。

  2. The meeting became productive once everyone got down to brass tacks.

    当大家开始谈关键问题后，会议才变得有效率。

02

go off the boil

UKINFORMAL

失去势头；热度或表现下降

  1. Sales went off the boil after the initial campaign ended.

    第一轮推广结束后，销售势头开始下降。

  2. The project went off the boil when the lead engineer left.

    首席工程师离开后，项目失去了推进势头。

03

break new ground

UK / USFORMAL

开创新领域；取得创新性进展

  1. The research team is breaking new ground in battery technology.

    研究团队正在电池技术领域取得创新性进展。

  2. The company broke new ground by offering the service entirely online.

    这家公司通过完全在线提供服务，开创了新的做法。

04

be in a pickle

UKINFORMAL

陷入困境；遇到麻烦

  1. We are in a pickle if the supplier cannot deliver by Friday.

    如果供应商周五前无法交货，我们就麻烦了。

  2. He got himself in a pickle by promising two clients the same delivery date.

    他向两个客户承诺了同一个交付日期，把自己弄得很被动。

05

go off the rails

USINFORMAL

失控；偏离正轨

  1. The project went off the rails after the scope changed for the third time.

    范围第三次变更后，项目就开始失控了。

  2. The discussion went off the rails when personal criticism began.

    当大家开始人身批评时，讨论就偏离了正轨。

06

on a shoestring

USINFORMAL

靠极少的预算

  1. They launched the first version on a shoestring budget.

    他们靠极少的预算推出了第一个版本。

  2. The charity runs on a shoestring but still reaches thousands of people.

    这个慈善机构预算很紧，但仍然服务了数千人。

07

by the book

UK / USINFORMAL

严格按照规则或流程

  1. The compliance team does everything by the book.

    合规团队所有事情都严格按流程办。

  2. We cannot improvise here; the procedure has to be followed by the book.

    这里不能临场发挥，流程必须严格执行。

08

throw good money after bad

UK / USNEUTRAL

在失败项目上继续投入，造成更大损失

  1. Continuing to fund the obsolete platform would be throwing good money after bad.

    继续给这个过时平台投钱，只会造成更大损失。

  2. They stopped the project because they did not want to throw good money after bad.

    他们叫停了项目，因为不想在失败的事情上继续砸钱。
